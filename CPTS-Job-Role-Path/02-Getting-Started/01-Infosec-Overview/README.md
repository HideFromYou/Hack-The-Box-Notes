# Infosec Overview

## Overview

Information security (infosec) is a vast field covering network and infrastructure security, application security, security testing, systems auditing, business continuity planning, digital forensics, and incident detection and response.

Infosec is the practice of protecting data from unauthorized access, changes, unlawful use, and disruption, while reducing the impact of any such incident. Data can be electronic or physical, tangible (e.g., design blueprints) or intangible (knowledge). The core tenet of infosec is information assurance: maintaining the **confidentiality, integrity, and availability (CIA)** of data.

## Learning Objectives

- Understand the scope of information security as a field
- Learn the five steps of the risk management process
- Understand the difference between red team and blue team
- Understand the role of a penetration tester

## Risk Management Process

| Step | Explanation |
|---|---|
| 1. Identifying the Risk | Identify risks the business is exposed to (legal, environmental, market, regulatory, etc.) |
| 2. Analyze the Risk | Determine impact and probability; map risks to policies, procedures, and business processes |
| 3. Evaluate the Risk | Rank and prioritize; decide to accept, avoid, control (mitigate), or transfer (insure) |
| 4. Dealing with Risk | Eliminate or contain the risk, working with the stakeholders of the affected system/process |
| 5. Monitoring Risk | Continuously monitor for situational changes that alter the impact score |

An incident can be a natural disaster, a system malfunction, or a security incident.

## Red Team vs. Blue Team

- **Red team** plays the attacker's role: penetration testing, social engineering, and other offensive techniques, to find weaknesses before real attackers do.
- **Blue team** makes up the majority of infosec jobs: strengthens defenses by analyzing risk, writing policy, responding to threats/incidents, and using security tools.

## Role of Penetration Testers

A security assessor (network pentester, web app pentester, red teamer) helps an organization identify risks in its external and internal networks: vulnerabilities, sensitive data exposure, misconfigurations, or issues that could cause reputational harm. A good tester identifies risks with the client, explains how to reproduce them, and provides mitigation/remediation guidance.

Assessment types range from a white-box penetration test of all in-scope systems, to a phishing assessment of security awareness, to a targeted red team engagement emulating a real-world threat actor. Understanding the bigger risk picture is essential to accurately rate vulnerabilities found during testing.

## Skills Practiced

## Key Takeaways

- Infosec's core goal is the CIA triad: confidentiality, integrity, availability.
- Risk management is a continuous 5-step process, not a one-time activity.
- Red team = offense, blue team = defense; most infosec jobs are blue team.
- A pentester's job isn't just finding bugs — it's explaining risk, reproduction steps, and remediation.
