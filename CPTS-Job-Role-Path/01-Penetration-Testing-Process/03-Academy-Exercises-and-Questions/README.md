# Academy Exercises and Questions

## Overview

Explains the design intent behind HTB Academy's exercises and questions, how to ask for help effectively when stuck, and shares guidance from the Academy team on approaching difficulty.

## Learning Objectives

- Understand how Academy exercises differ in intent from typical exam "trick questions"
- Build comfort with the ambiguity typical of real pentest engagements
- Know how to ask for help in a way that builds trust rather than just requesting answers

## Design Intent Behind Exercises

HTB Academy's exercises and questions are designed differently than typical exam trick questions — they aim to link knowledge with practice and build associative chains, always directly related to the material just taught. Difficulty is intentionally calibrated to the module's level and stated prerequisites.

Real pentests often come with vague, open-ended goals ("find as many vulnerabilities as possible in the given time"). The exercises are designed to build comfort with that ambiguity, not to trip students up.

## Asking for Help

Before asking "I can't solve X" or "tell me the answer," list out what's already known and what's already been tried. This often reveals the answer on its own, and it demonstrates effort to whoever ends up helping.

The same principle applies with a boss or manager in a real job: arriving with a partial solution and asking for validation — rather than asking for the answer outright — builds trust and reputation over time.

## Skills Practiced

- Framing a help request around what's already known and already tried
- Approaching open-ended, ambiguous exercises the way a real engagement would present them

## Key Takeaways

- Academy exercises intentionally mirror the ambiguity of real engagements rather than testing for a single "gotcha" answer — getting comfortable with open-ended goals here pays off directly in later client work.
- A help request that leads with "here's what I know and what I've tried" gets better answers and builds more credibility than one that just asks for the solution — in Academy and on the job alike.
- The repeated theme across the team quotes is that discomfort with difficulty is the mechanism of growth, not a sign something is wrong.
