# Laws and Regulations

## Overview

Surveys region-specific laws relevant to security research and penetration testing, so that engagements stay compliant and avoid civil or criminal liability, and closes with a precautionary-measures checklist to apply on every test.

## Learning Objectives

- Identify the major laws governing computer access and data protection in the USA, Europe, UK, India, and China
- Understand why written consent and scope adherence are legal necessities, not just best practice
- Apply a precautionary checklist before starting any engagement

## USA

| Law | Purpose |
|---|---|
| CFAA (Computer Fraud and Abuse Act) | Criminalizes unauthorized computer access; controversial for being broad/vague |
| DMCA | Prohibits circumventing copyright-protection technology measures, even for research purposes |
| ECPA | Regulates interception of electronic communications; requires consent |
| HIPAA | Governs protected health information handling |
| COPPA | Regulates collecting data from children under 13 |
| CISA | Critical infrastructure and personal data protection |

## Europe

| Law | Purpose |
|---|---|
| GDPR | Personal data handling; applies to any company processing EU citizens' data regardless of location; fines up to 4% of global revenue or €20M |
| NISD | Security measures + incident reporting for essential/digital service operators; applies to pentesters too |
| Cybercrime Convention of the Council of Europe | International cybercrime investigation cooperation framework |
| E-Privacy Directive | Electronic communications data processing within the EU |

## Precautionary Measures Checklist for Every Pentest

- Obtain written consent from the owner or an authorized representative of the system being tested
- Test only within the scope of that consent, respecting all limitations
- Take measures to prevent damage to systems/networks
- Don't access, use, or disclose personal data or other information found during testing without permission
- Don't intercept electronic communications without at least one party's consent
- Don't test HIPAA-covered systems/networks without proper authorization

## Skills Practiced

- Mapping applicable laws to a client's region before starting an engagement
- Applying a precautionary checklist as a gate before any testing begins

## Key Takeaways

- Legal frameworks differ significantly by region — a checklist that's compliant in one jurisdiction (e.g., USA) may not cover obligations in another (e.g., GDPR in the EU), so client location matters as much as client consent.
- GDPR's extraterritorial reach (applying to any company processing EU citizens' data, regardless of where that company is located) means EU data-protection obligations can apply even on a non-EU engagement.
- The precautionary checklist is short on purpose — it's meant to be run through before every single engagement, not just referenced once during training.
